\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis examined whether \fpos{} and \fmiss{}, the two components of a putative neuron's accuracy metric, could be predicted using real paired juxtacellular and intracellular ground-truth data. In doing so, it examined whether transfer learning from hybrid simulation data to paired juxtacellular and intracellular ground-truth data would be reliable and potentially enhance performance. To evaluate this, it was necessary to quantify the domain shift from hybrid simulation data to paired juxtacellular and intracellular ground-truth data. Additionally, the thesis also questioned the significance of recording-context data and whether it could provide a meaningful predictive signal for the two targets.

Our experiment showed that the domain shift from hybrid simulation data to paired juxtacellular and intracellular ground-truth data is significant, and that the two domains are almost perfectly separable using a simple logistic regression model \parencite{10.1111/j.2517-6161.1958.tb00292.x}. When predicting the two targets, \fpos{} and \fmiss{}, using only hybrid simulation data during training, our hybrid-only model collapsed completely for both \fpos{} and \fmiss{}. However, implementing transfer learning with a trust filter to retain only hybrid simulation units whose feature structure more closely matched that of paired ground-truth data improved performance in several settings, especially for \fpos{}, whereas gains for \fmiss{} remained much more limited. Context features also provided significant predictive value for certain models, particularly for \fpos{}, as evidenced by their contributions to the average SHAP value \parencite{lundberg2017unifiedapproachinterpretingmodel} of the \fpos{} model Waveform-augmented stack, whereas they did not offer an equally robust predictive signal for \fmiss{}.

Generally, we could not find a sufficiently robust model to reliably predict \fmiss{} across various settings. However, we identified models for \fpos{} that delivered notable predictive performance and accounted for a significant portion of the target variance.

Finally, a notable asymmetry was observed between \fpos{} and \fmiss{}. This indicates they should not be regarded as interchangeable parts of spike-sorting error, but rather as separate targets that may necessitate different features, datasets, and modelling approaches.

\section{Main Takeaway}

The main takeaway is that leakage-safe spike-sorting quality prediction under transfer from hybrid simulation data to paired ground-truth data is feasible for \fpos{}, not yet robust for \fmiss{}, and strongly conditioned by heterogeneity within paired ground-truth data. In practical terms, the project is strongest as a benchmark and analysis contribution: it shows where quality prediction works, where it remains fragile, and why the answer depends on both the biological regime and the evaluation regime.
