\section{The Transfer Problem Is Real}

The primary finding of the thesis is that the domain shift from hybrid simulation data to paired juxtacellular recording is significant. In fact, the two domains are easily separable: a domain classifier trained to differentiate source data from target data achieves an area under the curve of 0.9765 without recording-context features and 0.9997 with them. This indicates that the source and target domains are almost linearly separable based on the features available to the model.

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \caption{Domain separability between hybrid and paired data.}
    \label{tab:domain-separability}
    \begin{tabular}{llrr}
        \toprule
        Comparison & Feature set & Mean AUC & Std. dev. \\
        \midrule
        Hybrid vs paired (all) & Unit & 0.9765 & 0.0463 \\
        Hybrid vs paired (all) & Full context & 0.9997 & 0.0005 \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

This separability is supported by the broader distribution shift. Recording-context variables show a mean absolute standardized mean difference of about 0.518, waveform bins about 0.711, and the broader other-unit block about 0.734. This again highlights the complexity of paired recordings, which exhibit higher noise, contamination, and instability. H1 is therefore directly supported.

The two-dimensional principal-component projection confirms a similar pattern.

The paired target domain should therefore be thought of as a set of related sub-regimes rather than a compact distribution. ENGLISH spreads across most of the local PCA view, which is consistent with its biological diversity, as its recordings target three different brain regions: hippocampus, neocortex, and thalamus. KAMPFF occupies a more displaced region, highlighting a structural difference from other paired families. Finally, MEA64C\_YGER is relatively compact.

\section{\fpos{}: Positive Transfer Stories Exist}

\subsection{Twenty Recording-Disjoint Core Benchmark}

The main \fpos{} benchmark using the retained models was assessed using the recording-disjoint protocol across 20 seeds from 42 to 61. As expected, the dummy floor remains very close to zero at -0.0569 \rsq{}. The highest overall mean \rsq{} is achieved by the paired-only raw model with 0.4209. The hybrid-only source-transfer baseline drops to -1.8162, as anticipated following the significant domain shift identified above. The paired domain therefore contains notable predictive signal, but naive source transfer is unsafe.

Among the models combining source-side information, several closely match the paired-only raw \rsq{}, indicating effective transfer. However, while the score does not collapse, it does not deliver a universal improvement either. Paired-only context reaches 0.4188 \rsq{}, the context-first source stack 0.3973, the pre-waveform unlabeled structure stack 0.4068, and waveform-augmented structure 0.3906.

Taken together, these results support a positive transfer narrative for \fpos{} without indicating a universal model winner. Split-averaged stability matters as much as the strongest single split, because it conveys whether a recipe is robust. The meta-features therefore do matter, but in a regime-dependent way.

\section{\fmiss{}: The Retained Models Do Not Yet Deliver a Robust Transfer Story}

\subsection{Twenty Recording-Disjoint Core Benchmark}

The models compared in \fmiss{} differ from those studied in \fpos{}. This is intentional. We tried parallel transfer configurations, but several \fmiss{} variants collapsed or produced noisy predictions and were not informative. \fmiss{} is more global, more tied to the interaction with other putative units, and more dependent on firing dynamics. Using the exact same model family for both targets would therefore lead to non-informative comparisons. Each target is instead evaluated on the models that remained meaningful for it.

The retained \fmiss{} ladder is much weaker than the corresponding \fpos{} benchmark. Across the same twenty recording-disjoint seeds, the dummy floor is -0.0831 \rsq{}, the paired-only raw model is -0.2346, and the hybrid-only source-transfer baseline collapses to -2.2118. Adding paired-domain context partially repairs that failure: paired-only context rises to -0.0462 and reduced-latent context to -0.0128. This is an improvement over pure source transfer, but it is not a robust positive deployment story, because even the best retained mean \rsq{} remains slightly negative.

Paired-only context reaches -0.5544 and reduced-latent context falls further to -0.5733. \section{Cross-Target Asymmetry}

When the benchmark is summarised jointly across targets and protocols, the asymmetry becomes unmistakable. Under recording-disjoint evaluation, the best retained \fpos{} row is the paired-only raw baseline at 0.4209 \rsq{}, while the best retained \fmiss{} row is reduced-latent context at only -0.0128. Under leave-one-family-out evaluation, \fpos{} remains strongly positive, with the waveform-augmented structure model reaching macro \rsq{} 0.3975, whereas the least-bad aggregate \fmiss{} recipe falls to -0.3673. The gap between the two targets therefore widens under the harsher family-unseen protocol rather than narrowing.

This asymmetry is not only a leaderboard observation. The target-label structure is already different: \fpos{} is available for all packaged rows, while \fmiss{} is only defined when a valid ground-truth match exists. The biological information exposed by the current feature package is also different. Features such as contamination-like waveform irregularity, spread, and recording-relative context map more naturally onto \fpos{} than onto the mechanisms driving missed spikes. The benchmark therefore does not suggest one shared deployment story. It suggests two different problems.

The family-level comparison shows that this is not driven by a single outlier family. The same benchmark that yields broadly positive recording-disjoint \fpos{} performance still leaves the winning \fmiss{} line close to zero or negative on most families, while MEA64C\_YGER remains the clearest exception.

\section{Interpretability Supports the Context Story}

The explainability summary for the canonical waveform-augmented \fpos{} stack is consistent with the ablation results. Recording-context features contribute the largest mean absolute SHAP magnitude (0.1884), followed by SpikeInterface quality-control features (0.0928). Waveform features contribute 0.0341, source-transfer features 0.0318, autocorrelogram features 0.0276, and amplitude features 0.0161. Context is therefore not merely helping a little; it is the dominant explanatory block in the strongest canonical \fpos{} model.

There is no parallel frozen \fmiss{} SHAP export in the current manuscript assets. The \fmiss{} side of the asymmetry comparison is therefore represented by the reduced-latent context winner (\texttt{fmiss\_reduced\_latent}) in the family-behaviour and target-asymmetry figures rather than by a feature-level SHAP summary.

This matters because it shows that the model is not succeeding because one local waveform statistic suddenly becomes perfect. It succeeds because quality is partly a relational property: the same unit-level feature can mean different things depending on the statistics of the recording around it.

\section{Label Budget and Headroom}

The label-budget analysis confirms how constrained the problem is. With only 20 labeled target rows, \fpos{} performance is deeply unstable: the paired-context model averages -1.0124 \rsq{}, and the filtered context stack shows similar behaviour. At 60 rows, \fpos{} recovers to around 0.19 \rsq{}, and at the full 107-row canonical training budget it reaches 0.4873 for the paired-context model. \fmiss{} behaves similarly in terms of improvement with more labels, but its final robustness remains weaker despite some favourable canonical-fit numbers.

\section{Interim Resolution of the Hypotheses}

At the end of the Results chapter, all four hypotheses already have provisional answers.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{H1 supported.} Source and target domains are strongly shifted, and pure source transfer fails.
    \item \textbf{H2 supported.} Recording context consistently improves \fpos{} prediction and dominates the explainability summary.
    \item \textbf{H3 supported.} \fpos{} and \fmiss{} do not share one stable final modelling story.
    \item \textbf{H4 supported.} Family preference and evaluation regime both change the preferred \fpos{} recipe.
\end{itemize}

The next chapter turns these empirical observations into interpretation. Rather than repeating the numbers, it asks why the asymmetry appears, why waveform helps only in some regimes, and what can responsibly be claimed from a benchmark built on only 207 paired labels.
