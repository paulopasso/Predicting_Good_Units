# Interpretability And Limitations

The best `fpos` model remains interpretable enough to support a scientific discussion of what it learned. The main feature-importance summary in [fpos_shap_top.png](/Users/paulruiz/Documents/Predicting_Good_Units/thesis/figures/06_fpos_vs_fmiss_xai/fpos_shap_top.png) shows that the final model is still driven by a transferred prior plus corrective signals. In earlier diagnostics, `source_pred` was dominant, with amplitude-instability, waveform, and ISI-related features acting as the main correction layer. The thesis version of this argument should emphasize that the winning model is not “black-box waveform only”; it remains a structured transfer stack.

The family-level comparison between targets makes the asymmetry concrete. In [fpos_vs_fmiss_family_comparison.csv](/Users/paulruiz/Documents/Predicting_Good_Units/thesis/tables/06_fpos_vs_fmiss_xai/fpos_vs_fmiss_family_comparison.csv), some families are favorable for `fpos` and adverse for `fmiss`, while others invert that relationship. `PAIRED_KAMPFF` is especially striking: it is one of the best families for the `fpos` winner but strongly adverse for the best `fmiss` model. The combined figure [target_asymmetry_combined.png](/Users/paulruiz/Documents/Predicting_Good_Units/thesis/figures/06_fpos_vs_fmiss_xai/target_asymmetry_combined.png) should be used in the main text to show that this is not a subtle effect.

The robustness chapter should also make clear that the headline `fpos` winner is not the most stable model under family-unseen transfer. In [leave_one_family_aggregate.csv](/Users/paulruiz/Documents/Predicting_Good_Units/thesis/tables/03_paired_family_structure/leave_one_family_aggregate.csv), the waveform winner has slightly better macro MAE than the simpler reference stack, but a lower macro `R²` and a much worse worst-family `R²` (`0.1482` versus `0.2940`). This does not invalidate the main benchmark result; it shows that recording-disjoint optimization and family-unseen robustness are related but different targets.

Finally, the label-budget analysis shows that the benchmark is still data-limited. In [label_budget_summary.csv](/Users/paulruiz/Documents/Predicting_Good_Units/thesis/tables/07_limitations_and_data_budget/label_budget_summary.csv), both `fpos` and `fmiss` improve substantially as paired rows increase from `20` to `107`, and the curves in [label_budget_fpos.png](/Users/paulruiz/Documents/Predicting_Good_Units/thesis/figures/07_limitations_and_data_budget/label_budget_fpos.png) and [label_budget_fmiss.png](/Users/paulruiz/Documents/Predicting_Good_Units/thesis/figures/07_limitations_and_data_budget/label_budget_fmiss.png) do not look saturated. The most defensible future-gain claim is therefore not “more tuning,” but “better features and more target-domain information,” especially for spatial, template, and drift structure.
