# fmiss Results

`fmiss` behaves as a genuinely different transfer problem. The main benchmark ladder in [fmiss_benchmark_ladder.csv](/Users/paulruiz/Documents/Predicting_Good_Units/thesis/tables/05_fmiss_model_progression/fmiss_benchmark_ladder.csv) starts from a weaker baseline structure than `fpos`, and many methods that helped `fpos` do not help here. Pure hybrid transfer is strongly negative, and simple calibrated transfer only reaches `R² = 0.1515`. Even the first positive transfer baseline remains limited at `R² = 0.2128`.

The strongest `fmiss` gains come from a simpler, context-first LightGBM path. `Paired-only context` reaches `R² = 0.4158`, `Embed-nommd full context` reaches `R² = 0.4580`, and the final winner `Reduced-latent winner` reaches `R² = 0.4850` with `MAE = 0.1562`. This is the best `fmiss` result in the thesis package, and it confirms that `fmiss` favors a compact context-first representation rather than the heavier pseudo-plus-waveform recipe that wins for `fpos`.

The asymmetry is most visible in the failed transplant test. `Transplanted fpos waveform recipe` reaches only `R² = 0.3819`, clearly below the dedicated `fmiss` winner. This single row is important because it shows that the two targets should not be treated as the same task with different labels. The same source, context, and waveform ingredients interact differently with the two targets.

The ablation summaries reinforce that point. [fmiss_ablation_context.csv](/Users/paulruiz/Documents/Predicting_Good_Units/thesis/tables/05_fmiss_model_progression/fmiss_ablation_context.csv) shows that context is essential. [fmiss_ablation_unlabeled_paired_use_and_transferability.csv](/Users/paulruiz/Documents/Predicting_Good_Units/thesis/tables/05_fmiss_model_progression/fmiss_ablation_unlabeled_paired_use_and_transferability.csv) shows that the unlabeled-paired and transplanted `fpos` ideas are not the route to the best `fmiss` model.
